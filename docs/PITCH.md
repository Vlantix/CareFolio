# CareFolio — Session 1 pitch

Three minutes, five beats. If more than one of you presents, write a
name next to each beat, e.g. **[Speaker A]**.

## Script

### 1. The person (~45s)

My mother had a lab test done at the hospital. Then she lost the
papers: her prescription and her lab result.

So she went back to the hospital and asked for a copy. They told her to
file a formal request and come back in a few days.

She didn't. She paid to take the test again. She paid the fare again.
She queued again. All for a result the hospital already had.

### 2. Today (~30s)

That is how it works today. If you lose an official result, you travel
to the records office, fill in a paper form, wait days, and travel back
to collect it.

For an elderly patient, that is two trips and a lot of waiting. Often
it is a son or daughter who takes time off to do it for them. Or, like
my mother, people give up and pay for the test again.

### 3. Our fix (~45s)

CareFolio lets a patient, or a family member the hospital has linked to
them, request an official lab result from their phone.

They log in with their password and a code sent to their email. They
tap "Request a lab result", pick the test and the reason, and submit.

The records office sees the request, approves it, and attaches the
official PDF. The patient gets it on their phone. They can open it,
download it, and show it to a doctor, an employer or an insurer.

No trip. No queue. No paying for the same test twice.

### 4. The demo moment (~30s)

In Session 3, we will show the whole journey live, on a real phone.

A caregiver logs in and requests a lab result for their father. We
switch to the records office screen, approve it, and attach the PDF.
Then we go back to the phone, and the official result is there, ready
to download.

All of it uses fake patients. No real health data.

### 5. How we'll know (~30s)

Before Session 3, my mother will try it, along with classmates' parents,
brothers, sisters and grandparents.

We will watch whether they can request and download a result without
our help. We will time it, and we will write down every place they get
stuck. We will bring those notes to Session 3, good and bad.

## The three hardest questions

**1. "One story about your mother. Is this a real, common problem, or a
one-off?"** *(Our weakest point.)*

Honest answer: right now it is one story we watched closely. We have
not yet counted how often it happens. Before presenting, ask three to
five other families whether they have ever lost a result or redone a
test, and ask the records office how many copy requests they get each
week. Even one real number makes this answer far stronger.

**2. "Would the hospital actually use this? They refused your mother a
copy."**

Honest answer: they didn't refuse outright. They wanted a formal request
and a wait, and CareFolio *is* the formal request, just online. But we
have not spoken to a records office yet; so far only a doctor has said
it's a good idea. Before presenting, try to get ten minutes with one
records-office staff member and ask: "If requests arrived like this,
would you release copies this way?"

**3. "Health data on a student-built website. How do you keep it
safe?"**

Honest answer: every login needs a password and a code emailed to you.
The database itself checks that code, so a stolen password alone is not
enough. Each person only sees the patients the records office has linked
them to. The demo uses fake patients only, because real health data is
protected under the Data Privacy Act. We are honest that a real launch
would need the hospital's privacy officer to review it.

## Strengthen before you present

The problem rests on one family's story, and no records office has
agreed to take part yet. Two small things would change how this pitch
lands:

1. **A number.** Ask three to five families or classmates whether they
   have ever had to redo a test or chase a record.
2. **A voice from the other side.** Ask one records-office staff member
   whether they would release copies this way.
